Installation in Houston

How walk-in tub installation works in Houston

A good install is mostly planning. This page walks through each step, what the City of Houston expects for permits, and how to check that the plumber and electrician are licensed.

The steps, from first call to first bath

  1. Phone conversation. We ask who will use the tub, what is hard today, what may change in the next few years, and a little about the bathroom.
  2. Measuring. We record the alcove, the doorway into the bathroom, the path from the front door, the drain end and the location of the water heater and electrical panel. Our fit check shows what we look at.
  3. Choosing the tub. Door direction, seat height, drain speed and any jets are picked to fit the person and the plumbing.
  4. Written quote and scheduling. The quote lists the tub, removal, plumbing, electrical, wall finish and permits.
  5. Removal. Floors and the path through the house are protected and the old tub or shower is taken out and hauled away.
  6. Setting the tub. The tub is leveled so the door seals and the water drains fully, then supply and drain are connected.
  7. Electrical, if needed. Jets and heaters are connected to their dedicated circuits.
  8. Walls and finish. Surround panels or tile go up, edges are sealed.
  9. Test and walkthrough. We fill and drain the tub with you, show you the door and controls, and answer questions.

Many installs are finished in a single day once the tub is on site. Jobs that move a drain on a slab, add circuits or repair hidden water damage can take longer, and the quote should say so.

City of Houston permits: what to know

The Houston Permitting Center handles building, plumbing and electrical permits inside the city limits, which include neighborhoods such as Kingwood, Clear Lake and the Energy Corridor. A few points from the city's own pages are useful for a bathroom project:

  • Plumbing permits are bought by a registered master plumber. The city says a plumbing permit must be purchased by a Responsible Master Plumber registered with the City of Houston.
  • Homeowner permits exist, with limits. The city allows a homeowner's plumbing permit when the owner lives in the home, has it registered as a homestead and does the plumbing work personally.
  • Bigger remodels need a remodel permit. When a project goes beyond swapping fixtures, such as reworking walls or adding a bathroom, a residential remodel permit with plan review and trade inspections may apply.
  • Simple swaps can be exempt. The city's winter-storm repair guidance notes, for example, that replacing a faucet does not need a permit.

Whether your specific tub replacement needs a permit depends on what changes. Moving a drain, adding a circuit or opening walls are the usual triggers. When in doubt, call the Permitting Center before work starts. Suburbs such as Sugar Land, Pearland, League City and Conroe have their own building departments, and unincorporated areas such as Cypress and parts of Spring follow Harris County or Montgomery County rules, so the answer can differ a few miles away.

Texas licensing: what to check

Texas does not issue a general contractor license, so a "contractor" can be anyone. The trades are a different story:

  • Plumbers are licensed by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. You can look up a license number on the board's site.
  • Electricians are licensed by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, which also has a public lookup.

Ask any installer, including us, who will do the plumbing and electrical work and what their license numbers are. It is a fair question and a good company answers it without fuss.

Install-day tips for the family

  • Clear the hallway and bathroom, and move cars so a truck can park close to the door.
  • Plan for the bathroom to be out of use for the day, and longer if tile or drain work is involved.
  • If the person who will bathe can be there for the walkthrough, ask them to sit in the tub, practice the door and reach the controls while installers are still there.
  • Keep pets in another room. Doors will be open and tools will be out.

Supply-only orders

If you have your own licensed plumber, we can ship the tub supply-only with tech support. Your installer should read the manufacturer's install guide before the tub arrives, confirm the drain side and plan the electrical work. Most families still choose full installation so one team is responsible from start to finish.

Questions we hear about this

Do I need a permit to replace a bathtub with a walk-in tub in Houston?

It depends on what changes. Moving drain lines, adding electrical circuits or opening walls usually brings in permits from the Houston Permitting Center, and the city requires plumbing permits to be purchased by a registered master plumber. Ask before work begins.

How long does walk-in tub installation take?

Many installs finish in a single day once the tub is on site. Drain changes on a slab, new circuits or hidden water damage can add time.

Does Texas license general contractors?

No. Texas has no statewide general contractor license, but plumbers are licensed by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners and electricians by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.
