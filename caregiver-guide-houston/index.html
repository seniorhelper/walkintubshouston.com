A caregiver guide to safer bathing in the Houston area

Bathing is private, and the talk about it can be hard. Here is how to start, what to fix first, and where Houston caregivers can find support.

Direct answer: Start with quick fixes: grab bars, a shower chair, a handheld shower and a water heater set at 120°F or lower. If bathing is still a struggle, or you find yourself lifting the person, it is time to plan a walk-in tub or roll-in shower. Harris County caregivers can call the Area Agency on Aging at 832-393-4301; nearby counties use H-GAC at 1-800-437-7396.

Signs bathing has become hard

Older adults often stop taking baths quietly rather than admit they are afraid of getting stuck. Signs to notice gently:

  • Bathing less often, or only when someone else is home.
  • A dry tub and damp washcloths at the sink.
  • Holding the towel bar, sink or shower door to steady themselves.
  • Bruises, a recent fall, or a new fear of falling.
  • Recovery from a hip, knee or back surgery.

How to bring it up

Lead with what the person wants: staying in their own home, keeping their routine, not needing help. "I want you to be able to keep taking your bath the way you like" lands better than "the tub is dangerous." Ask what feels hard. Let them try a shower chair or a grab bar before deciding on anything bigger. If a doctor or therapist suggests changes, people often accept them more easily than advice from family.

Fix the quick things this week

  1. Grab bars, anchored into studs or with rated anchors, at the tub entry and beside the toilet. Towel bars are not grab bars.
  2. Nonslip mats inside the tub and a rubber-backed rug outside.
  3. A shower chair or transfer bench and a handheld shower.
  4. Night lighting from the bed to the bathroom.
  5. Water heater at 120 degrees Fahrenheit or below, as the National Institute on Aging suggests.

When a bigger change makes sense

If those steps are in place and bathing is still a struggle, it may be time for a walk-in tub, a walk-in shower or a roll-in shower. A good rule: if the person cannot safely lift a leg over the tub wall even with a bench and grab bar, or if you are physically lifting them, the bathroom itself needs to change. Our tub or shower guide walks through the choice.

Helping during a bath without hurting yourself

  • Get everything ready first: towels, clothes, soap and the handheld shower within reach.
  • Check the water temperature with your wrist or a thermometer.
  • Let the person do what they can. Guide, do not lift.
  • In a walk-in tub, stand where you can reach the door and the drain control. Outward doors give a helper more room.
  • Never leave someone who is unsteady alone in deep water.
  • Keep the floor dry and the exhaust fan running. In a humid Houston bathroom, puddles last.

Support for Houston-area caregivers

Caring for a parent is work, and help exists.

  • Harris County Area Agency on Aging (Houston Health Department): caregiver support, benefits counseling, in-home services and referrals for people 60 and older in Harris County. Call 832-393-4301.
  • H-GAC Area Agency on Aging: the same kinds of help in Fort Bend, Montgomery, Brazoria, Galveston and eight other surrounding counties. Call 1-800-437-7396.
  • 2-1-1 Texas: dial 2-1-1 for local help with home repairs, transportation and more.
  • Michael E. DeBakey VA Medical Center: if your parent is a veteran, ask about the caregiver support program and HISA home modification grants.

Questions we hear about this

How do I know if my parent needs a walk-in tub?

If grab bars, a bench and a handheld shower are in place and they still cannot safely step over the tub wall, or you are lifting them, the bathroom itself likely needs to change.

Where can Houston caregivers get support?

The Harris County Area Agency on Aging (832-393-4301) and the H-GAC Area Agency on Aging (1-800-437-7396) offer caregiver support and referrals. 2-1-1 Texas also connects families to local help.

Can I arrange the project if I live out of town?

Yes. We can talk with you by phone, send the quote to you, and coordinate measuring and installation with your parent or a local contact.
