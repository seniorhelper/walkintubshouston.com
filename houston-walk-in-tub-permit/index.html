Permits in Houston

Do you need a permit to install a walk-in tub in Houston?

Who is allowed to pull the plumbing permit, what the City of Houston charges, when a remodel permit and plan review kick in, and what changes outside the city limits. Updated November 8, 2026.

Call 1-888-779-2284

Our installers pull the permits the job needs. You get a written quote that lists them.

Direct answer: Yes, inside Houston city limits a walk-in tub install normally runs on a plumbing permit, and in most cases the licensed plumber pulls it. Houston issues plumbing permits to a Responsible Master Plumber registered with the city; a homeowner can buy one only for a registered homestead they live in and work on themselves. The city's January 2026 homeowner form lists a total minimum permit fee of $131.12, inspection required.

In short: a straight tub-for-tub swap is a plumbing permit with an inspection. Add a dedicated circuit for jets or a heater and an electrical permit joins it, pulled by a registered master electrician. Open or move walls and the job can become a residential remodel permit with plan review. Outside the city limits the rules change, and we say plainly below which parts we could not confirm.

Who is allowed to pull a plumbing permit in Houston?

The Houston Permitting Center, run by Houston Public Works, handles plumbing permits inside the city limits. Two paths exist for a bathroom job:

  • A licensed plumber pulls it. This is the normal route for a walk-in tub. The city registers plumbing contractors and issues their permits to a Responsible Master Plumber, the designation the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE) gives a master plumber who takes responsibility for a business and carries the required insurance. The plumber applies, schedules the inspection and keeps the permit on site.
  • A homeowner's plumbing permit. The city's homeowner permit page says the owner must own and occupy the home as a registered homestead, must do the plumbing work personally, and must have all work inspected under the current plumbing code. You apply in person at 1002 Washington Ave., 4th floor, with form CE-1284, a driver's license or proof of homestead and payment. Processing is listed as one business day, the permit is valid for 180 days, and plan review is not required.

The homeowner route is real, but it is for people who will set the tub, connect the supply and drain and face the inspector themselves. A rental, a second home or a parent's house you do not live in does not qualify. If a company installs the tub, the permit is theirs to pull, and a good installer puts the permit on the quote. Our Houston installation guide shows where the permit sits in the schedule.

When does a residential remodel permit and plan review apply?

The city's residential remodel permit (HPWCODE1135) covers remodels, conversions and additions. It is a building permit, not a trade permit, and it brings plan review: a floor plan of the existing building showing the affected area, wall sections that identify bearing and non-bearing walls, materials, and header and beam details if a load-bearing wall comes out. The page lists processing at 1 to 15 business days, a six-month validity period and trade inspections scheduled by the registered licensed contractor.

A walk-in tub that drops into the old tub alcove does not touch that list. Projects that do tend to involve one of these:

  • Removing or moving a wall to fit a longer or wider tub, or to join a closet to the bathroom.
  • Converting the tub space to a roll-in shower with the floor opened for a new drain location, which is common on Houston's slab foundations.
  • Adding a bathroom, or changing a half bath into a full bath.

When one of those is in play, the remodel permit sits on top of the plumbing and electrical permits, and the timeline stretches. Ask the installer which permits the quote assumes and who is named on each.

Do jets or an inline heater need an electrical permit?

Usually, yes. Air jets, water jets and inline heaters run on dedicated circuits, and a new circuit is electrical work. On its electrical permit pages the city states that electrical permits are only issued to registered master electricians, and that contractors must register their master and contractor licenses with the city first. We did not find a homeowner option for electrical permits on the city's site, so plan on a licensed electrical contractor for that part. The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) licenses electricians and has a public license search.

A soaker tub with no pump or heater may need no electrical work at all, which is one reason to decide on features early. Our features guide explains what each option adds, electrically and otherwise.

What if the home is in unincorporated Harris County?

Much of Cypress, parts of Spring, Klein and Atascocita sit outside any city. There the Harris County Office of the County Engineer issues permits. Its FAQ says a permit is needed for all development in the county, defined broadly as any man-made change to real estate, and its floodplain rules are strict: an elevation certificate in the 500-year floodplain, and engineer- or architect-prepared drawings in the 100-year floodplain.

What we could not confirm: whether the county requires a permit for an interior fixture swap with no change to the footprint. The county's permits A-to-Z list names residential construction, septic systems and water wells, and does not list a residential plumbing, electrical or interior remodel permit. We are not going to guess, so call the Permits office at (713) 274-3900 (1111 Fannin St., 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.) with the address before work starts. State trade licenses apply everywhere in Texas regardless of local permits. If the home has flooded before, read our flood and hurricane planning guide first; floodplain rules can shape what you are allowed to build.

Incorporated suburbs such as Sugar Land, Pearland, League City, Pasadena and Conroe run their own building departments with their own forms and fees. Our city pages link to each one.

Does the HOA have a say?

Interior work inside your own walls is rarely the association's business, but the parts that happen outside can be. Typical covenants speak to work hours, dumpsters and material staging in the street, contractor parking and, in condominiums, shutting off a shared water riser. Townhome and condo associations often ask for notice before plumbing work and may require that the plumber be licensed and insured. Read the covenants, send a short note to the manager with the date and the plumber's license number, and keep the reply with your permit paperwork. None of that replaces the city permit; it runs alongside it.

What this means for your walk-in tub

If a licensed installer does the work, the permit question mostly answers itself: the plumber's company pulls the plumbing permit, an electrical contractor pulls the circuit permit if jets or a heater are involved, and you are handed the permit numbers. If you plan to do the plumbing yourself in your own homestead, budget $131.12 and a trip downtown, and expect an inspector to look at your work. Either way, confirm the jurisdiction first, since the right office changes at the city line.

Houston permit questions we hear

Can I pull my own plumbing permit for a walk-in tub in Houston?

Only if you own and live in the home as your registered homestead and you do the plumbing work yourself. The city's homeowner plumbing permit requires an inspection, is applied for in person with form CE-1284, and is valid for 180 days. If a company installs the tub, its Responsible Master Plumber pulls the permit instead.

How much is the permit for a tub replacement in Houston?

The city's January 2026 homeowner plumbing form lists a total minimum permit fee of $131.12, made up of a $97.56 minimum fee and a $33.56 administration fee. Plumbing fixtures are listed at $34.24 for the first three. A re-inspection costs $94.00. The older web page still shows $116.11, so confirm the figure when you pay.

Do I need an electrical permit for a walk-in tub with jets?

A new dedicated circuit for jets or an inline heater is electrical work, and the City of Houston says electrical permits are only issued to registered master electricians. Expect a licensed electrical contractor to pull that permit. A plain soaker tub with no pump or heater may not need electrical work at all.

Does a tub-to-shower conversion need a remodel permit?

It can. The residential remodel permit covers remodels and conversions and brings plan review, with processing listed at 1 to 15 business days. A conversion that opens the slab for a new drain or moves a wall is more likely to need it than a tub that drops into the existing alcove. Ask the installer which permits the quote assumes.

Do I need a county permit in Cypress or Spring?

Unincorporated addresses fall under the Harris County Office of the County Engineer, which says a permit is needed for all development and enforces floodplain rules. We could not find a county page that settles whether an interior tub swap needs a county permit, so call (713) 274-3900 with the address before work starts.

What happens if the tub is installed without a permit?

Unpermitted plumbing can surface at resale, during an insurance claim or when a later inspector finds it. The practical fix is to ask for the permit number before install day and keep it with the inspection record.
