How much does a walk-in shower cost in Houston?

Two kinds of numbers show up when you search, and they describe different jobs. Angi's Houston guide reports an average of about $7,569 for shower installation, with most homeowners spending between $2,855 and $12,450, and lists tub-to-shower conversions at $1,070 to $7,290. Those figures cover ordinary showers: a new pan or tile, a door, a valve.

An accessible shower is a different build. It has a threshold low enough to roll or shuffle over, a pan sloped so water stays inside without a tall curb, a seat, grab bars in the right places, a handheld shower on a slide bar and controls you can reach sitting down. Walk-in, roll-in or transfer shower: which one fits the person?

Start with how the person moves today and how they are likely to move in five years. A walk-in shower suits someone who walks steadily with or without a cane and only needs to stop stepping over a tub wall. A roll-in shower suits a wheelchair or rolling shower chair user, or anyone who shuffles and cannot lift a foot. A transfer shower is a smaller, square space for someone who slides from a wheelchair onto a built-in seat.

Private homes do not have to meet the ADA, but the 2010 ADA Standards are a useful yardstick for sizes, and the US Access Board's guide spells them out:

What changes when you replace a bathtub with a shower in a Houston house?

The slab decides the drain

Most Houston-area houses are built on concrete slabs. A tub drain sits at one end of the alcove; a shower drain usually sits in the center or along a trench at the back. If the new pan can use the old drain position, the plumbing hookup is quick. If the drain must move, the installer saws and breaks out concrete, re-routes the line and pours a patch. That is the single biggest swing in labor, so we ask for a photo of the tub drain end before quoting.

Garden tubs in suburban primary baths

Many primary bathrooms in Katy, Cypress, Spring and League City subdivisions were built with a deck-mounted garden tub and a separate small shower. Those garden tubs are hard to climb into and rarely used once knees or hips complain. Taking out the deck often frees enough floor for a roomy roll-in shower, and the old shower stall can stay or become storage. Aging Safely Baths has a separate guide to converting a garden tub to a walk-in shower that covers the steps and options in more depth.

Humidity, mold and what is behind the old surround

A tub surround that has leaked for years in Gulf Coast humidity can hide soft drywall or rotted framing. Nobody can see it until the old tub is out, so a fair quote explains in writing how hidden damage will be handled and priced. A new shower should also have a working exhaust fan; our flood, hurricane and humidity planning guide covers fans, materials and storm prep.

Hard water and the glass

Houston water leaves spots and scale on glass and chrome. A curtain on a roll-in shower avoids glass cleaning altogether and leaves a wider opening for a chair. If you want glass, plan on a daily squeegee. More in Houston hard water and your tub.

Permits

Inside Houston city limits a like-for-like swap normally runs on a plumbing permit pulled by the licensed plumber, and moving walls can trigger a residential remodel permit with plan review. Our Houston permit guide lists the current fees and who pulls what. Outside the city, your city or the county handles it, and we confirm before scheduling.

Can VA HISA or STAR+PLUS help pay for a shower in Houston?

Possibly, and the order of steps matters because programs rarely pay for work already finished.

  • VA HISA. The VA's Home Improvements and Structural Alterations grant names roll-in showers as an example of an essential bathroom change. It is a lifetime benefit of $6,800 for service-connected conditions (or some non-service-connected conditions for veterans rated 50 percent or more) and $2,000 for other conditions. Projects must be medically justified, and spa or hot tub features are excluded. The Prosthetics office at the Michael E. DeBakey VA Medical Center is where Houston veterans start.
  • STAR+PLUS. Texas Medicaid's STAR+PLUS handbook lists modifications for accessible bathroom facilities, including wheelchair accessible showers, with a lifetime limit of $7,500 per member and $300 a year for repairs. The health plan must finish an approved modification within 90 business days. Your service coordinator makes the request.
  • Medicare. Original Medicare generally does not pay for showers or walk-in tubs. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer home safety benefits; ask your plan in writing.

Our Houston funding guide covers these programs, local repair grants and the IRS medical deduction. We help you check what may apply; we never promise coverage.
