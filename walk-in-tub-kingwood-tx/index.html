Kingwood homeowners planning to stay

Kingwood was built as a wooded planned community northeast of Houston and later became part of the City of Houston. Many residents have lived here for decades, raising families along the greenbelt trails, and now want a bathroom that works for the years ahead. We also help families caring for a parent nearby in Humble, Atascocita and Porter.

We talk first, measure, and put the whole job in writing before anything is ordered.

Kingwood bathrooms

Original villages

Homes in the older villages often date from the 1970s and 1980s. Many have hall baths with a standard alcove and primary baths with a tub-and-shower combination. Original tile and drywall behind the tub may hide old leaks, so our quote explains how we handle repairs if we find them.

Newer sections

Later villages have larger primary baths with garden tubs and separate showers. These rooms offer flexibility: a walk-in tub where the garden tub was, a roll-in shower where the old shower stood, or both.

Flood history around Lake Houston

Kingwood sits along the San Jacinto River and Lake Houston, and Harvey in 2017 flooded thousands of homes in the Kingwood and Lake Houston area. Many families rebuilt bathrooms after that storm. If yours flooded, tell us. We will look at the subfloor and wall cavity and may suggest moisture-tolerant surround materials. If you are planning a bigger remodel, ask the city whether its post-Harvey floodplain elevation rule applies. Our flood and storm guide explains the basics.

Permits: Kingwood follows City of Houston rules

Because Kingwood is inside Houston city limits, the Houston Permitting Center handles building, plumbing and electrical permits. The city requires plumbing permits to be purchased by a registered master plumber, with a limited exception for homeowners doing their own work on a homestead. Moving drains, adding circuits or opening walls are common triggers. Humble, Atascocita and Porter are different jurisdictions with their own rules.

Local help for older adults

Kingwood is in Harris County, served by the Harris County Area Agency on Aging through the Houston Health Department at 832-393-4301. It can help with benefits counseling, caregiver support, meals and referrals. Porter and other addresses across the Montgomery County line are served by the H-GAC Area Agency on Aging at 1-800-437-7396. Veterans can ask the Michael E. DeBakey VA Medical Center about HISA grants.

Choosing well

For a longtime Kingwood resident who loves a warm bath, a walk-in tub with a heated seat and fast drain can be a daily pleasure. For someone who uses a walker, a low-threshold shower may be safer. We will help you choose based on the person, not the catalog.

Getting ready

Kingwood's tree-lined streets can make parking and access tight. Let us know about a narrow driveway, gate codes or a side entry that would make carrying the tub easier.

Ask about access panels

When a tub is set against an interior wall, an access panel in the next room can let a plumber reach the drain and pumps later without opening tile. We look for that chance in every Kingwood install, since many homes here have a hall closet or bedroom wall behind the tub.

Kingwood's water: hard, and its own system

Kingwood homes inside the city get water from the City of Houston's separate Kingwood system. Its 2025 water quality report lists total hardness of 124 mg/L as calcium carbonate, in the U.S. Geological Survey's "hard" range. That means mineral scale on a walk-in tub's door seal, faucet and jets, and sediment in the water heater, are worth keeping up with. Our hard water guide has a simple routine.

Because Kingwood is part of the City of Houston, the Census Bureau counts its residents in the city's figures: about 277,100 Houstonians, 12.0%, were 65 or older in the 2019 to 2023 American Community Survey.
