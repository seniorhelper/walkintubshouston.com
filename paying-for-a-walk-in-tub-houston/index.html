Paying for a walk-in tub in the Houston area

Most families pay for a walk-in tub themselves, but some get real help. Here is what each program actually says, where Houston residents apply, and what to do before you sign.

Medicare: usually not, and here is why

Original Medicare (Parts A and B) pays for durable medical equipment from a defined list, things like walkers, hospital beds and commode chairs. Bathroom safety items, including walk-in tubs, are generally not on that list, so Original Medicare does not usually pay for them. Medicare.gov's equipment page is the place to check the rules yourself.

Medicare Advantage plans are run by private insurers and some offer extra benefits for bathroom safety, such as grab bars or a shower seat. Plans in Harris, Fort Bend and Montgomery counties differ, and structural changes like a new tub are usually excluded. Call the number on the member card and ask the plan in writing what it will and will not cover before you count on anything.

If anyone tells you "Medicare will pay for your tub," ask them to show you the exact rule. We will not make that promise.

Veterans: the HISA grant through the Houston VA

The VA's Home Improvements and Structural Alterations (HISA) grant helps eligible veterans pay for medically necessary changes to their primary home, such as making a bathroom accessible. According to the VA, the lifetime limit is up to $6,800 when the work is for a service-connected condition (or for a non-service-connected condition when the veteran has a service-connected rating of 50 percent or more), and up to $2,000 for other enrolled veterans.

In the Houston area, the process runs through the Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service at the Michael E. DeBakey VA Medical Center and its clinics. A VA provider has to document the medical need, you apply with VA Form 10-0103, and the VA generally will not reimburse work done before the grant is approved. HISA does not cover spa or hot tub installations, so ask your VA team how they view a jetted model before choosing one. Eligible veterans may qualify; the local VA Prosthetics office makes the decision.

Texas and Houston-area help

Amy Young Barrier Removal Program (TDHCA)

The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs offers one-time grants of up to $22,500 for households that include a person with a disability and need accessibility changes or removal of hazards at home. Household income cannot exceed 80 percent of the area median family income, and both owners and renters can apply. TDHCA does not take applications directly; local nonprofits and governments run the program, and it is not offered everywhere. Use TDHCA's page to find whether an administrator covers your part of the Houston region.

Medicaid home and community programs

Texans enrolled in certain Medicaid long-term services programs can sometimes get minor home modifications through their health plan's service coordinator. Eligibility, limits and approvals are set by the state and the plan, so ask the service coordinator directly. Our sister guide for statewide programs covers this in more depth; here we keep to the Houston steps.

Your Area Agency on Aging

If the person lives in Harris County, start with the Harris County Area Agency on Aging, run by the Houston Health Department, at 832-393-4301. In Fort Bend, Montgomery, Brazoria, Galveston, Waller, Chambers, Liberty and the other surrounding counties, the H-GAC Area Agency on Aging serves older adults at 1-800-437-7396. These agencies offer benefits counseling and care coordination and can point you to local repair or modification help. They are referral resources, not guaranteed funders of a tub.

Taxes: a possible medical expense

IRS Publication 502 explains that home improvements made mainly for medical care can count as a medical expense, reduced by any amount the change adds to your home's value. It lists bathroom changes for a person with a disability among the examples. The deduction only helps if you itemize and your medical costs pass the income threshold. Keep the doctor's recommendation and your invoices, and talk with a tax professional before you count on it.

Do things in the right order

  1. Get a written recommendation. A note from a doctor or an occupational therapist helps with VA, Medicaid and tax questions.
  2. Call the program first. Many programs will not pay for work already finished. Ask what quote format they need.
  3. Get a detailed quote. We can provide a written quote listing the tub, labor and each extra so an agency can review it.
  4. Wait for approval in writing before scheduling, if a program is involved.
  5. Keep every document. Approvals, invoices and photos help if questions come up later.

Questions we hear about this

Will Medicare pay for a walk-in tub in Houston?

Original Medicare generally does not, because walk-in tubs are not on its covered equipment list. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer limited bathroom-safety benefits, but structural changes are usually excluded. Ask your plan in writing.

How do Houston veterans apply for a HISA grant?

Talk with your VA provider about the medical need, then work with the Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service at the Michael E. DeBakey VA Medical Center. You apply with VA Form 10-0103, and approval should come before any work starts.

Who runs the Area Agency on Aging for Harris County?

The Harris County Area Agency on Aging is run by the Houston Health Department and can be reached at 832-393-4301. Surrounding counties are served by the H-GAC Area Agency on Aging at 1-800-437-7396.

Is a walk-in tub tax deductible?

It can be a medical expense under IRS Publication 502 if its main purpose is medical care, reduced by any increase in home value, and only if you itemize. Ask a tax professional.
